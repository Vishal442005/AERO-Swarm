C ONCLUSIONS AND L IMITATIONS

We introduced ESNN, a first-order Cartesian framework that enriches equivariant message passing
through structured matrix-valued transport of vector features across graph edges. ESNN increases
geometric expressivity in the edge interactions without requiring higher-order representations. We
characterize displacement-conditioned linear O(n)-equivariant transport and show how learned
covariant features enable richer feature-conditioned transformations. We also introduce controlled
symmetry relaxation for systems with a preferred ambient direction, preserving exact equivariance to
the remaining symmetry while recovering full E(n)-equivariance as a special case. Across diverse
geometric learning problems, ESNN improves predictive performance over first-order equivariant
baselines, learns a preferred direction from data, and remains robust to unseen rotations. The current
formulation is limited to scalar and first-order vector features; the completeness result applies only to
displacement-conditioned linear transport, and symmetry relaxation assumes a single global preferred
direction. Future work could extend ESNN to richer representations, local or gauge-equivariant
transport, and more general symmetry-breaking fields.

A central distinction among E(n)-equivariant graph networks lies in the geometric representations
they propagate and how these representations are coupled across neighboring nodes. One broad
family works directly with invariant scalars and low-order Cartesian features. EGNN (Satorras et al.,
2021) constructs coordinate updates from invariant edge functions acting on relative displacement
vectors, while PaiNN (Schütt et al., 2021) and GVP-based models (Jing et al., 2021) maintain
scalar and vector channels coupled through equivariant operations. Subsequent architectures have
expanded this Cartesian design space through richer scalar–vector interactions (Wang et al., 2024b),
multiple vector channels (Levy et al., 2023), and Cartesian tensor features (Simeon & De Fabritiis,
2023). More recent models such as GotenNet (Aykent & Xia, 2025) similarly seek greater geometric
expressivity while avoiding explicit Clebsch–Gordan contractions. A complementary family uses
steerable features transforming under higher-order group representations. Tensor Field Networks
(Thomas et al., 2018), SE(3)-Transformers (Fuchs et al., 2020), SEGNN (Brandstetter et al., 2022),
MACE (Batatia et al., 2022), and EquiformerV2 (Liao et al., 2024) represent angular structure through
spherical harmonics and tensor-product or related steerable operations. Higher representation degrees
can capture increasingly rich angular information, but also increase the cost of the representations
and their couplings. Recent work has therefore investigated how much high-degree information is
needed and how it can be processed more efficiently (Cen et al., 2024). ESNN instead retains a
first-order scalar–vector representation and increases geometric expressivity through matrix-valued
transport of vector features across graph edges.

Another route to equivariance is to resolve orientation dependence through the choice or averaging
of reference frames. Frame Averaging (Puny et al., 2022) constructs exactly equivariant models
by averaging a backbone over an equivariant frame, with FAENet (Duval et al., 2023) adapting
this idea to atomistic systems. Learned canonicalization (Kaba et al., 2023) instead predicts a
canonical representative before applying a generic backbone, while subsequent work has examined
the benefit of retaining tensorial messages beyond canonicalized scalar representations (Lippmann
et al., 2025). Local gauge equivariance addresses a related but distinct setting, in which different
sites may use independently chosen reference frames. Gauge-equivariant CNNs (Cohen et al., 2019)
and coordinate-independent convolutions (Weiler et al., 2021) require the network to transform
consistently under changes of these local frames. Tangent-bundle convolutional networks connect this
viewpoint to connection Laplacians and cellular sheaves through vector-field diffusion on Riemannian
manifolds (Battiloro et al., 2024), while Torsor CNNs extend local group-valued transport to arbitrary
graphs through edge potentials relating neighboring frames (Li et al., 2026). These models concern
local gauge equivariance, whereas ESNN enforces equivariance under a common ambient O(n)
action. Its Orthogonal Transport therefore has a connection-like geometric interpretation, but is not
a general gauge-equivariant construction. A separate line of work extends Euclidean equivariance
to richer combinatorial domains. E(n)-Equivariant Topological Neural Networks (Battiloro et al.,
2025) generalize equivariant message passing from graphs to combinatorial complexes, while E(n)equivariant message-passing cellular networks (Kovač et al., 2024) develop related constructions on
cellular structures. These approaches enrich the underlying domain through higher-order cells. ESNN
is complementary: it remains graph-based and instead enriches the transport between geometric
feature spaces. A related theoretical perspective is provided by (Maruyama, 2026), who formulates
order-equivariant neural networks on equivariant vector bundles over face posets and recovers graph
and sheaf layers within a common framework. Their construction organizes equivariance over the
