A BSTRACT
Equivariant graph neural networks constrain how geometric features transform, but
efficient first-order architectures remain limited in how vector information can be
transported across graph edges. We introduce ESNN, an Equivariant Sheaf Neural
Network that learns directed, matrix-valued transport between neighboring vector
features while preserving exact Euclidean equivariance. Rather than increasing
representation order, ESNN retains first-order scalar and vector features and places
additional geometric expressivity in the edge interactions themselves. Its transport
mechanisms range from isotropic transformations to feature-conditioned rotations
and anisotropic radial–tangential actions, allowing vector messages to be transformed according to the local geometry before aggregation. We also equip ESNN
with a controlled symmetry-relaxation mechanism for systems with a preferred
ambient direction, preserving exact equivariance to the remaining symmetry while
recovering full E(n)-equivariance as a special case. Across diverse geometric
learning problems spanning physical dynamics, point clouds, and molecular systems, ESNN improves predictive performance over first-order equivariant baselines,
learns a latent preferred direction from data, and remains robust to unseen rotations.
These results establish edge-wise geometric transport as a source of equivariant
expressivity that does not require higher-order representations.

I NTRODUCTION

Many physical systems, from molecular dynamics to fluid mechanics, are naturally represented as
geometric graphs embedded in Euclidean space (Satorras et al., 2021; Brandstetter et al., 2022; Du
et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b; Aykent & Xia, 2025). Their states may contain invariant scalar attributes together with vectors or tensors that transform with the geometry (Schütt et al., 2021; Simeon
& De Fabritiis, 2023; Liao et al., 2024). Equivariant neural networks encode these transformation
laws directly, ensuring predictable behavior under translations, rotations, and, when appropriate,
reflections. Equivariance alone, however, does not determine how geometric information should be
transformed as it passes between neighboring nodes. Many interactions are intrinsically directional:
longitudinal and transverse responses, shear, and anisotropic propagation depend on local orientation
rather than on distance alone. Existing geometric architectures capture this structure in different
ways. Cartesian models work efficiently with invariant scalars and first-order vectors (Satorras et al.,
2021; Schütt et al., 2021), while steerable models enrich the propagated representations through
higher-order features, spherical harmonics, and tensor products (Thomas et al., 2018; Batatia et al.,
2025; 2022). We instead retain a first-order scalar–vector representation and increase expressivity
through richer geometric transport of vector features. This perspective is closely related to Sheaf
Neural Networks (SNNs), where interactions between local feature spaces are mediated by matrixvalued maps (Hansen & Gebhart, 2020; Bodnar et al., 2022). Generic sheaf maps, however, are
not inherently constrained to respect the transformation laws of geometric vector features. ESNN
addresses this by constraining matrix-valued edge transport to satisfy Euclidean equivariance. Each
transport separates an O(n)-covariant spatial action from invariant mixing across vector channels,
allowing the geometric transformation applied to a message to depend on the local interaction while

Our main contributions are summarized as follows: (i) We introduce Equivariant Sheaf Neural
Networks, a first-order Cartesian framework that learns matrix-valued transport of vector features
across graph edges while preserving E(n)-equivariance. (ii) We develop a family of equivariant
transport mechanisms that progressively enrich how vector features interact across edges, from
identity and isotropic transport to feature-conditioned orthogonal transformations and anisotropic
radial–tangential transport. (iii) We extend ESNN to systems with a preferred ambient direction, while
retaining exact equivariance to the corresponding stabilizer subgroup, and evaluate the framework
across particle dynamics, mesh-based physical simulation, point-cloud classification, and molecular
property prediction.

R ELATED W ORK AND BACKGROUND

Related Work. ESNN combines ideas from equivariant message passing and sheaf-based graph
learning. Cartesian architectures such as EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021) and scalar–vector models such
as PaiNN and GVP (Schütt et al., 2021; Jing et al., 2021) achieve efficient E(n)-equivariance using
invariant scalars together with low-order covariant features. Steerable architectures instead propagate
higher-order representations and couple them through equivariant tensor products (Thomas et al.,
2018; Fuchs et al., 2020; Brandstetter et al., 2022; Batatia et al., 2022; Liao et al., 2024). ESNN
remains in the first-order scalar–vector setting, but increases geometric expressivity by enriching
the transport applied to vector features rather than by introducing higher-order representations. The
transport viewpoint is closely related to SNNs, where scalar edge weights are replaced by linear maps
between local feature spaces (Hansen & Gebhart, 2020; Bodnar et al., 2022). Subsequent work has
explored more structured forms of transport, including orthogonal connection maps for geometric
propagation (Barbero et al., 2022a) and directed and asymmetric sheaf propagation (Ribeiro et al.,
2026; Fiorini et al., 2026). Closely related copresheaf formulations place similar emphasis on directed
maps and their composition across the graph (Hajij et al., 2025). ESNN adopts this matrix-valued view
of message transport, but constrains the learned maps to respect the Euclidean transformation laws of
geometric vector features. A complementary line of work studies settings in which external structure
reduces the symmetry of the problem. Subequivariant models enforce the stabilizer of a prescribed

an underlying symmetry (Wang et al., 2022; Hofgard et al., 2024). ESNN incorporates a preferred
direction within the transport framework, retaining exact stabilizer equivariance and recovering full
E(n)-equivariance when the directional conditioning is inactive. Further discussion of related work
is provided in Appendix A.
