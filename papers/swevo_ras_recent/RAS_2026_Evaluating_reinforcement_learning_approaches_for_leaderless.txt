Title: Evaluating reinforcement learning approaches for leaderless UAV swarms in obstacle-laden environment

--- ABSTRACT ---
Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) swarms provide huge potential for complex missions, yet coordinating large-scale collectives in obstacle-laden environments without a single global leader remains a significant challenge. While Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has become a dominant paradigm for such coordination, systematic comparisons of how different training architectures perform within hierarchical manager-worker swarm topologies that deliberately eliminate the top-level aggregator remain absent from the literature. This study compares centralized (CTDE), decentralized (DTDE), and federated (FTDE) training approaches, all with decentralized execution, for a leaderless hierarchical UAV swarm of 18 agents organized into three manager-worker clusters. All paradigms are implemented using Asynchronous Proximal Policy Optimization (APPO) and evaluated over a fixed ten-session training budget. Centralized learning achieves the highest final reward, while federated learning attains the highest cumulative reward over the training budget by avoiding a mid-training performance drop observed in the centralized approach. Federated learning is also the only approach whose reward stays within a stability band through the end of the training budget, reaches a final collision rate comparable to that of centralized learning, and reduces inter-cluster training communication by approximately 80% relative to centralized learning. Under simulated manager failure, decentralized and federated learning each retain over 94% of nominal performance, compared to 63.9% for centralized learning. A theoretical analysis shows that the inter-cluster communication cost of the federated approach is independent of the number of workers per cluster when the number of manager clusters is fixed, although it grows with the number of clusters. Validation with an alternative algorithm (MAPPO) preserves the ordering of the three approaches on both final and cumulative reward, and an obstacle-density study preserves the final-reward ordering. These results indicate that peer-to-peer federated learning provides a balanced trade-off between learning performance, communication overhead, fault tolerance, and scalability for hierarchical UAV swarms.
