Related Cheeger inequalities. Liu, Münch and Peyerimhoff [36] prove Buser-type inequalities
for connection Laplacians with orthogonal or unitary matrices, bounding eigenvalues from above
under nonnegative Ricci curvature. Their Cheeger constants are mixtures of the expansion rate of
the underlying graph and the frustration index of the signature. One open question would be to
generalize Ricci curvature for Connection Laplacian to Bochner matrices, and, subsequently, apply
analogous Cheeger inequalities to Bochner matrices.
For general sheaves, First and Kaufman [64] prove Cheeger-type inequalities bounding the coboundary
expansion of sheaves obtained as quotients of a constant sheaf by a subsheaf. Their technique applies
only to certain graphs, which they characterize, whereas the inequalities above hold for every Bochner
matrix on an arbitrary graph. To our knowledge, the Bochner matrices defined in this work are
therefore the largest class of cellular sheaves for which a Cheeger inequality is known on arbitrary
graphs.

Forman Sheaf Curvature

So far we have presented the Bochner–Weitzenböck decomposition for each order k of the Hodge
Laplacian of a simplicial complex, highlighting that even the node-graph case k = 0 is non-trivial.
We then considered Bochner matrices, the first term arising from the Bochner decomposition, and
studied them on their own, setting aside the decomposition they come from. In this section we turn
back to the simplicial complex setting, and to the k = 1 case. Throughout this section we write
H1F = B1F + F1F for the Bochner decomposition of the 1-dimensional Hodge sheaf Laplacian, to keep
it apart from the graph sheaf Laplacian LF of the previous sections. We show that the sheaf Forman
term, which we call sheaf Forman Ricci curvature RicF := F1F , controls the first sheaf cohomology
dimension, dim H1 (M ; F), when it is positive semidefinite everywhere. This is the analogue of the
Bochner theorem for cellular sheaves, and we obtain it by generalizing Forman’s original techniques
to our setting. Throughout, M is a compact, connected, quasiconvex regular CW complex and F has
invertible restriction maps, everywhere of dimension d.
